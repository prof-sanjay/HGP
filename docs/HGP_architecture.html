Part A. Horizon-specific causal graph generation

Built once from training rows, then fixed. One pass per horizon.

A.1 Road-restricted ridge VAR

\[ x_t=\sum_{k=1}^{3}A_k\,x_{t-k}+e_t,\qquad (A_k)_{ij}=0\ \text{unless}\ j\in\mathcal N(i)\cup\{i\}. \]

Each target row \(i\) is fitted separately by ridge regression on its allowed regressors \(X_i\) (lags 1 to 3 of the sources), using only rows where target and regressors are observed:

\[ \hat\beta_i=\big(X_i^{\top}X_i+\lambda I\big)^{-1}X_i^{\top}y_i,\qquad \lambda=10^{6}. \]

The ridge biases magnitudes toward zero. Rankings and supports are what the pipeline uses.

A.2 Impulse responses and standardised effect

\[ \Psi_0=I,\qquad \Psi_s=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(s,3)}A_k\,\Psi_{s-k}. \]

\(\Psi_h[i,j]\) is the reduced-form effect on sensor \(i\), \(h\) steps later, of a unit innovation at sensor \(j\). No shock identification is imposed, so the quantity is a predictive (Granger-type) effect, not a structural one. With \(\sigma_i\) the standard deviation of sensor \(i\):

\[ a_h(i,j)=\lvert\Psi_h[i,j]\rvert\,\frac{\sigma_j}{\sigma_i},\qquad a_h(i,i)=0. \]

A.3 Candidate pool

Let \(U_k=\{k\}\cup\mathcal N(k)\) (neighbours in either direction). Sensor \(j\) is a candidate source for target \(i\) when they share a unit:

\[ P_{ij}=\mathbb 1\big[\exists k:\ i,j\in U_k\big],\quad i\neq j, \qquad \mathcal C_i=\{j:P_{ij}=1\}. \]

This is the set of sensors within two undirected road hops. On PEMS-BAY \(\lvert\mathcal C_i\rvert\) averages about 34. The pool is the only place where an edge the road-restricted VAR cannot represent can enter.

A.4 Group-knockoff selection (target \(i\), horizon \(h\))

Rows and pre-processing. Time-of-day means are removed per sensor (training rows only). Rows \(t\in\{h+2,\ h+5,\ h+8,\dots\}\) are used, one every 15 minutes, to weaken serial dependence. With \(L=3\) own lags:

\[ y_t=x_i(t),\qquad o_t=\big(x_i(t-h),\dots,x_i(t-h-L+1)\big),\qquad X_t=\big(x_j(t-h)\big)_{j\in\mathcal C_i}. \]

Own history removed. Both \(y\) and every candidate column are residualised on \([1,\,o_t]\) by least squares, then standardised, so the statistic measures what the sources add beyond the target's own past. Let \(\tilde y\) and \(\tilde X\) be the results and \(n\) the number of complete rows (at least 2000 are required).

Correlation and groups. With shrinkage 0.01,

\[ \hat\Sigma=0.99\,\tfrac1n\tilde X^{\top}\tilde X+0.01\,I,\ \text{rescaled to unit diagonal}. \]

Candidates are clustered by average linkage on \(1-\lvert\hat\Sigma_{jk}\rvert\), cut so members of a group have \(\lvert\hat\Sigma_{jk}\rvert\ge 0.5\) on average. Let \(\mathcal G\) be the groups and \(\Sigma_{\mathcal G}\) the block-diagonal part of \(\hat\Sigma\).

Second-order Gaussian group knockoffs. With \(S=\gamma\,\Sigma_{\mathcal G}\) and \(\gamma\) the largest value in \((0,1]\) keeping \(2\hat\Sigma-S\succeq 0\),

\[ \tilde X^{\mathrm{ko}}=\tilde X-\tilde X\hat\Sigma^{-1}S+E\,V^{1/2},\qquad V=2S-S\hat\Sigma^{-1}S,\quad E_{ab}\sim\mathcal N(0,1). \]

The knockoffs copy the within- and between-group correlations of the real candidates but carry no information about \(\tilde y\) beyond them.

Statistic. A lasso path of \(\tilde y\) on \([\tilde X,\ \tilde X^{\mathrm{ko}}]\) gives, per column \(j\), the largest penalty \(\lambda_j\) at which it enters (0 if it never does). For a group \(g\):

\[ W_g=\max\!\Big(\max_{j\in g}\lambda_j,\ \max_{j\in g}\lambda^{\mathrm{ko}}_j\Big)\cdot\operatorname{sign}\!\Big(\max_{j\in g}\lambda_j-\max_{j\in g}\lambda^{\mathrm{ko}}_j\Big). \]

Knockoff+ threshold at level \(q=0.3\):

\[ \tau=\min\Big\{t\in\{\lvert W_g\rvert\}:\ \frac{1+\#\{g:W_g\le -t\}}{\max\big(1,\#\{g:W_g\ge t\}\big)}\le q\Big\}. \]

Groups with \(W_g\ge\tau\) are selected. One representative per group is kept (the member with the largest individual statistic), and at most 25 sources per target are retained. The selected set is \(\mathcal S_h(i)\). Targets with no group above \(\tau\) receive no sources.

Guarantee and its limit. Under the model-X assumptions (known covariate law, exchangeable rows), knockoff+ gives \(\mathbb E[\mathrm{FDP}]\le q\) over the selected groups. On traffic data both assumptions hold only approximately: the covariate law is replaced by its second moments and rows are serially dependent. The guarantee is therefore approximate.

A.5 Graph assembly

The partial correlation of the \(h\)-step relation uses the shrunk correlation matrix \(C\) of \([x_i(t{+}h),\,x_i(t),\,x_{\mathcal C_i}(t)]\) (shrinkage 0.02), \(\Omega=\big((1-0.02)C+0.02I\big)^{-1}\):

\[ \rho_h(i,j)=\frac{\lvert\Omega_{0j}\rvert}{\sqrt{\Omega_{00}\,\Omega_{jj}}}. \]

Edge weights before scaling, for \(j\in\mathcal S_h(i)\):

\[ \tilde G_h[i,j]=\begin{cases}a_h(i,j) & a_h(i,j)>0\ \ (\text{VAR supports the edge})\\[2pt] \kappa_h\,\rho_h(i,j) & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \qquad \kappa_h=\frac{\operatorname{median}\{a_h:\text{supported}\}}{\operatorname{median}\{\rho_h:\text{added}\}}. \]

One global factor \(c_h\) makes the mean non-zero weight equal to that of the VAR-only reference graph \(G_h^{\mathrm{VAR}}\) (the 25 largest \(\lvert\Psi_h\rvert\) per target):

\[ G_h=c_h\,\tilde G_h,\qquad c_h=\frac{\operatorname{mean}\{G^{\mathrm{VAR}}_h>0\}}{\operatorname{mean}\{\tilde G_h>0\}}. \]

Part B. HICP: horizon-indexed causal propagation

All three horizons come out of one forward pass.

B.1 Input

Per sensor and step, the input is the standardised flow (per-sensor training mean and standard deviation; short gaps filled by the gap-band policy, flagged) and four time features shared by all sensors:

\[ u_{t,i}=\big[\,x_{t,i},\ \sin2\pi\tfrac{\mathrm{tod}}{288},\ \cos2\pi\tfrac{\mathrm{tod}}{288},\ \sin2\pi\tfrac{\mathrm{dow}}{7},\ \cos2\pi\tfrac{\mathrm{dow}}{7}\,\big]\in\mathbb R^{5},\qquad t=1,\dots,12. \]

B.2 Shared per-sensor encoder

\[ Z_i=\operatorname{Dropout}\!\big(\operatorname{GRU}(u_{1:12,i})_{12}\big)\in\mathbb R^{d},\qquad Z\in\mathbb R^{B\times N\times d}. \]

One GRU with the same weights for every sensor. \(Z\) holds no cross-sensor information and no sensor-ID embedding.

B.3 Graph branches

Every graph is row-normalised over its incoming edges and has no self-loop (the self path is \(Z\)). For a graph \(M\):

\[ \hat M=D_M^{-1}M,\qquad D_M=\operatorname{diag}\!\Big(\sum\nolimits_j M_{ij}\Big). \]

Branch \(m\in\{c,p,s\}\) (causal, physical, semantic) at horizon \(h\) is one directed hop with its own weights:

\[ S^{m}_h=\operatorname{Dropout}\!\Big(\operatorname{GELU}\big(\hat A^{m}_h\,Z\,W^{m}_h+b^{m}_h\big)\Big),\qquad W^m_h\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}. \]
BranchGraph \(A^m_h\)Shared across horizons?
causal \(c\)\(A^c_h=G_h\) from Part Ano, one graph per horizon
physical \(p\)\(A^p=R\) symmetrised, binary, no self-loopsgraph yes, weights \(W^p_h\) no
semantic \(s\)\(A^s\) belowgraph yes, weights \(W^s_h\) no

The semantic graph is learned from the forecasting loss only. It is not causal and not computed from correlations. With learned node embeddings \(E_t,E_s\in\mathbb R^{N\times 16}\) and \(K=25\):

\[ A^{s}[i,j]=\operatorname{softmax}_{j\in\operatorname{top-}K_i}\!\Big(\tfrac{E_t[i]\cdot E_s[j]}{\sqrt{16}}\Big),\qquad j\ne i,\ \text{all other entries }0. \]

B.4 Horizon-conditioned gate

A learned embedding \(e_h\in\mathbb R^{8}\) per horizon conditions a gate shared across horizons. For \(M\) active branches the logits and weights are, per node and feature:

\[ \ell_h=\operatorname{MLP}\big([\,Z,\ S^{c}_h,\ S^{p}_h,\ S^{s}_h,\ e_h\,]\big)\in\mathbb R^{M\times d},\qquad \alpha_h=\operatorname{softmax}\big([\,0,\ \ell_h^{c},\ \ell_h^{p},\ \ell_h^{s}\,]\big). \]

The MLP is Linear\(\big((1+M)d+8,\ d\big)\), GELU, Linear\(\big(d,\ Md\big)\). The self logit is fixed at 0. A branch that is absent gets \(-\infty\). The mixed representation is convex:

\[ H_h=\alpha^{0}_h\odot Z+\sum_{m}\alpha^{m}_h\odot S^{m}_h,\qquad \alpha^{0}_h+\sum_m\alpha^m_h=1. \]

With only the causal branch, \(\operatorname{softmax}([0,\ell])\) equals a sigmoid gate and \(H_h=a_h S_h+(1-a_h)Z\). The mean of \(\alpha^m_h\) per horizon is logged as a readout of branch use.

B.5 Heads and loss

\[ \hat y_h=H_h\,w_h+b_h\in\mathbb R^{B\times N},\qquad \hat y_h\leftarrow\hat y_h\,\sigma+\mu\ \ (\text{de-standardised for scoring}). \]

With \(\Omega_h\) the observed (window, sensor) pairs for horizon \(h\), the loss is a masked MAE with equal horizon weights:

\[ \mathcal L=\frac1{\lvert\mathcal H\rvert}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H}\frac1{\lvert\Omega_h\rvert}\sum_{(b,i)\in\Omega_h}\big\lvert\hat y_{h}[b,i]-y_{h}[b,i]\big\rvert. \]

B.6 Training

AdamW, learning rate \(3\times10^{-4}\), weight decay \(10^{-4}\), batch 16, one warm-up epoch then cosine decay, gradient-norm clip 5, at most 40 epochs, early stopping on validation mean MAE over 15 / 30 / 60 min (patience 6). The test period is scored once per run. The model with physical and semantic branches at \(d=128\) has 327,483 parameters, the same for the family and no-graph runs.
